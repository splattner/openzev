This is the largest release so far: 42 changes. Two of them matter more than the rest. A common-area meter can now split itself across every participant instead of being billed to one person who divides it by hand. And a tariff can be entered the way Swiss grid operators actually publish it — imported from the operator's own file, priced by season, and carrying as many time bands as it really has.

**If you run the Docker Compose stack, read [Upgrading](#upgrading) before pulling.** The database moves to a new location inside its volume, and the first start after this release begins with an empty database until you restore.

### Common-area meters split themselves across participants

A shared meter — stairwell, lift, laundry, a common connection — is usually held by one participant on paper. OpenZEV billed the whole thing to that person, who then divided it outside the system: invisible to everyone else, and one invoice where there should have been one share each.

Set an assignment's **Allocation** to **Community** when you assign the metering point, and every participant's invoice arrives already carrying their share of that meter — its energy, its per-kWh levies and its per-metering-point fees.

Shares follow each participant's **allocation weight**. An equal split is simply the case where every weight is the same, not a separate setting, so you configure one thing rather than choosing between two. Weights are renormalised across whoever is actually active on the day energy was used, or in the month a fee applies — so somebody joining or leaving mid-period changes the split from that point rather than retroactively.

Worth being clear about what allocation weight is *not*: it is a positive, unitless number, not a percentage, not a per-mille value, and not a Swiss STWE *Wertquote*. If your community allocates by Wertquote, enter the quotas as weights; the arithmetic works out the same, but nothing in OpenZEV claims a legal equivalence it cannot hold.

### Import your grid operator's published tariffs

Since 2025 every Swiss grid operator has to publish its grid-usage, energy, metering and public-authority tariffs in machine-readable form each year (Art. 7b StromVV). Until now you transcribed those numbers out of a PDF by hand, once a year, and a wrong grid fee propagated into every invoice.

Enter the operator's address once under **ZEV settings → Grid connection**, then on the **Tariffs** page choose **Import from grid operator**. You get a preview of every tariff in the document — its price, its validity, and its status against what you already have — and you tick the ones that apply to your community. Nothing is written until you confirm.

The preview is the point. An operator's document describes every customer group it serves; the one this was built against carries 23 published tariffs and offers 35 candidates, of which a community needs three or four. The operator's own "standard product" flag is pre-ticked and everything else is left to you.

Two things it asks rather than assumes. A published base price is an amount per month, but the document cannot say *who* pays it — so each fee row has a picker: a fee shared across the community, a fee each participant pays, or a fee per metering point, which is what a metering charge usually is. And the standard does not label its time bands, so where the import has to guess which is the high tariff it says so on the row rather than deciding quietly.

Next year's document appends a new version to each tariff and closes the previous one. Re-importing the same document does nothing at all.

Anything the document contains that OpenZEV cannot yet bill — a power/demand charge, a dynamic tariff pointing at an external price feed — is listed with the reason. Nothing is dropped silently.

### Tariffs can be entered the way they're published

Two shapes that previously had to be approximated by hand now go in as they stand.

**Seasonal prices.** A band can apply in only some months, so a winter/summer tariff is entered once instead of being swapped twice a year. Seasons combine with high and low tariffs, so a tariff carrying winter-HT, winter-NT, summer-HT and summer-NT is four bands and four prices in one tariff.

**More than two bands.** High and low are the two bands a Swiss tariff traditionally has, and they were the only two OpenZEV could store. A peak/shoulder/off-peak tariff now goes in as three bands, each with an optional name — left blank, a band is shown by its time window, which is what tells it apart anyway.

Both appear on the participation contract with their season and window named, so a contract states which half of the year a price is for rather than printing one number and leaving the reader to guess.

The months and weekdays a band applies on are now picked from toggles rather than typed as comma-separated numbers, which removes having to know that Monday is `0` while January is `1`.

### The app and its documents look like one product

The web interface and the printed documents had drifted into two visual languages — a blue app, forest-green PDFs. They now share one set of design tokens by construction rather than by anyone remembering, and the interface has been rebuilt around readable hierarchy and calmer density.

The participation contract got the same treatment: page numbers, a document id and issue date in the footer, and every value the system already knows pre-filled instead of printed as a blank line to complete by hand. It also gains a summary appendix and a binding privacy notice.

PDF previews now embed the real document bytes. The old preview wrote HTML into an iframe, where browsers ignore page geometry and margin boxes — so it could never match what actually printed. Invoices have a preview on their detail page for the first time.

Underneath, this was a simplification rather than a re-theme. One of the three styling systems is gone entirely — with it the MUI data grid and a second date picker — and the frontend ends with less styling code than it started with.

### Contracts are issued, not just downloaded

Downloading a participation contract now creates a numbered, retained snapshot of exactly what was handed over. Contracts are signed instruments, so "which version did this participant actually receive" needs an answer that does not depend on regenerating the document later and hoping nothing changed in between.

Each ZEV keeps its own contract counter. Asking again returns the same frozen snapshot; a new numbered version is minted only when the underlying data or the template actually changed.

Issuing is now a `POST`, because an action that creates a record of itself is not a read. `GET` still fetches a contract — it streams the latest issued version and never mints one, which means it now returns 404 for a participant whose contract has never been issued. If you script against this endpoint, see Upgrading.

### Also new

- **Pick your grid operator from the official list.** The grid operator field suggests from ElCom's published register of Swiss operators, so the same utility stops arriving as "EKZ", the full name, and three typos. Any operator missing from the register is still enterable.
- **Documents moved to Reports.** Annual statements, financial summaries and contract downloads now live on **Reports** instead of being mixed into the dashboard, which is back to being an overview.
- **The dashboard's open-invoice list filters on the server** rather than in the browser, so it stays correct however many invoices a community has.
- **More of the interface is translated**, including confirm-dialog defaults, participant validation messages and chart tooltips.

### Fixes worth knowing about

- **The Docker Compose database had no durable storage.** The named volume was mounted at a path the Postgres image does not use, so the real cluster landed in an anonymous volume — discarded whenever the container was recreated. If you run Compose, your development database was not being kept. Fixed, with a one-time move described under Upgrading.
- **Invoice PDFs were reachable without logging in.** Two paths served documents straight off `/media/` with no authentication: the application's own URL handling, and the Compose nginx configuration. Both now require an authenticated request.
- **Paginated lists could drop or duplicate rows.** Several models had no unique tiebreaker in their ordering, so rows tying on the sort key could land on two pages or none. Now that the frontend walks every page rather than reading only the first, "the complete list" could quietly be missing an entry.
- **A deactivated meter changed what got billed.** The billing engine consulted a metering point's active flag when pricing a period, so deactivating a meter silently altered historical invoices. Whether a meter is active is a statement about today, not about the period being billed.
- **Weight splits could reach outside a tariff's own validity**, producing shares that did not add up to the amount being divided.
- **The annual feed-in total rounded differently** from the other kWh totals it sits beside, so a statement could disagree with itself in the last decimal.
- **A tariff's split key was lost** when you created a new version or duplicated it, quietly reverting a shared fee to an equal split.
- **Owners of more than one community could not switch between them.**
- **A security pass** closed several holes: cookie-based sessions now enforce CSRF, the user list is admin-only, OAuth client secrets are write-only, uploads are hardened against zip bombs and parse loops, the template preview can no longer be used to fetch arbitrary URLs, and the public authentication endpoints are rate-limited.

### Under the hood

Shared-meter allocation now runs through a single read model, so community totals, invoices, annual statements and dashboards can no longer disagree about the same number. The frontend finished migrating to one styling system and a shared page contract, and gained a guard that keeps the brand colour ramp monotonic. The test suite runs in parallel; the invoice list stopped serialising nested items and email logs it never displayed; and a `.dockerignore` took the build context from 930 MB to 8 MB.

### Upgrading

**If you run the Docker Compose stack, do this before pulling.** The Postgres cluster moves to a pinned subdirectory of its volume, because the previous mount put it in a location the image discards on recreate. The first start after this change initialises an empty database — your existing volume still holds its data at the old path. Dump before upgrading and restore afterwards; the sequence is in the README and in `docs/user-guide/01-getting-started.md`.

This does not affect Helm or Kubernetes deployments, which bring their own database.

**Sixteen migrations, no configuration changes.** Nothing rewrites existing data:

| App | Count | What |
|---|---|---|
| `invoices` | 4 | New `ContractIssue` table for issued contract snapshots; `PdfTemplate.default_digest` added and stamped on existing override rows; two ordering changes |
| `zev` | 5 | Four new nullable fields — contract counter, assignment allocation mode, ElCom operator id, tariff source URL — and one ordering change |
| `tariffs` | 4 | Three new fields on tariffs and price bands — split key, months, band label — and one ordering change |
| `accounts`, `audit`, `metering` | 3 | Ordering changes only |

Seven of the sixteen only change model ordering and emit no SQL at all. No downtime is expected.

**No removals, one API behaviour change.** Nothing that worked in 1.7.0 has been taken away, but `GET /api/v1/zev/participants/<id>/contract-pdf/` no longer generates a contract as a side effect of being read. It streams the latest issued version and returns 404 if none exists; `POST` to the same path issues one. A script that relied on `GET` producing a document on demand needs one `POST` first.
