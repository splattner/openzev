This release extends the dynamic tariffs added in 1.13.0 to the way most Swiss operators actually pay for exported energy: the greater of a guaranteed minimum and the federal reference market price. Nothing about an existing tariff changes until you set a minimum on it.

### Feed-in at whichever is higher

A feed-in tariff priced from a fetched series can now carry a **Minimum price (CHF/kWh)**, on the tariff form under **Setup → Tariffs**. Every exported kWh is credited at the greater of that minimum and the fetched price, interval by interval.

The displayed average is floored the same way, per interval and before weighting, so the figure on the Tariffs page — which gains `min. CHF 0.080/kWh` beside the average — cannot disagree with what the invoice bills. The tariff overview PDF prints both, as `, at least 8.00 Rp./kWh` after the rate: the minimum is knowable even for a period whose reference price is not yet.

The minimum belongs to a tariff *version*, so renegotiating it means a new version from the date it changes, like any other price. It applies only to a feed-in tariff priced from a series — a floor on a grid tariff would propagate into the percentage-of-energy base that such tariffs feed, which is not what an "at least X" feed-in clause means. There is no ceiling, and a period the series does not cover is still refused rather than billed at the minimum, since under-crediting a producer whose reference price later turns out to be higher is the worse error.

### The BFE reference market price as a price source

The dynamic source dialog now asks for a **Source kind**: an operator endpoint as before, or the **BFE reference market price**. The federal publication is a small open-data CSV rather than an endpoint to probe, so there is nothing to discover and no URL to enter — pick the **Publication series**, quarterly or monthly, and the **Technology** (Solar (PV), Hydropower, Wind power or Biomass). OpenZEV downloads and stores the whole published history, back to Q3 2023, the moment you save.

It is one shared source per series and technology, listed with the rest under **Platform administration → Overview → Dynamic prices** — a national reference price is one fact, not one per community.

Two things to plan around. **A quarter can only be billed once BFE has published it**, roughly ten working days after the quarter ends; until then readiness names the uncovered days and invoice generation refuses. And if BFE ever revises a past period that a non-cancelled invoice already billed, the fetch fails as a whole, which also stops newly published periods arriving — the source sits in `failed` with the conflict named, and the fix is to resolve the affected invoice and clear the source's prices rather than wait for the next refresh.

The figures are federal open data under Opendata BY. Reusing them outside OpenZEV — a newsletter, your own report — requires naming the source, the Swiss Federal Office of Energy.

### Also new

- Opened from **Setup → Tariffs**, **View prices** is now scoped to the tariff you opened it for: the range defaults to and is bounded by that tariff's own validity, and points below its minimum are shown at the minimum. A shared source's series often begins years before a given tariff linked to it. The admin panel, which has no single tariff to scope to, still shows the raw shared series.

### Under the hood

ADR 0018 now records why the reference price is a third publication protocol on the existing price-source tables rather than a parallel model — evidence retention, the protection of prices a live invoice has billed, and every admin operation therefore apply to it unchanged. `docs/specs/2026-09-bfe-reference-market-price.md` covers the parser, the unit conversion and the display rules, and the user guide gains a section on setting the pair up.

### Upgrading

Two migrations, both in **tariffs** and both additive: a nullable minimum-price column, and a widened set of source-protocol choices. No data is rewritten and no downtime is expected.

No configuration changes and nothing removed. Existing tariffs are untouched until a minimum is set, and operator-endpoint sources are unaffected in fetching, display and price history — their price history keeps its 31-day range cap, which is lifted only for the period-published reference price.
